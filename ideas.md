# Morrow & Roast — Website Design Direction

## Three Possible Approaches

### 1. Dawn Ledger

**Very Brief Intro:** A warm editorial storefront inspired by independent coffee journals and morning newspapers. It makes product discovery feel thoughtful, tactile, and quietly premium rather than transactional.

**Probability:** 0.07

### 2. Market Pantry

**Very Brief Intro:** A lively, ingredient-led shop that uses hand-drawn labels, dense product shelves, and earthy colour fields. It would feel local, generous, and slightly playful.

**Probability:** 0.04

### 3. Quiet Workshop

**Very Brief Intro:** A spare workshop aesthetic built from dark materials, product photography, and technical brewing notes. It would foreground expertise and precision.

**Probability:** 0.09

## Chosen Direction: Dawn Ledger

### Design Movement

The site adopts a **contemporary editorial commerce** aesthetic, drawing on the restrained grids, typographic confidence, and tactile restraint of independent food journals. This is not a conventional e-commerce catalogue; it should feel like an invitation to build a better morning ritual.

### Core Principles

1. **Editorial hierarchy over generic cards:** Information should be led by serif headlines, strong rules, and deliberate spacing rather than repeated panels.
2. **One clear commercial action per moment:** The user should always understand the next step, especially in the hero, product selection, and cart tray.
3. **Tactile warmth with technical clarity:** Product stories can feel crafted, while prices, variants, and cart states remain sharply legible.
4. **Mobile-first confidence:** The desktop page should remain an expanded version of a coherent 375px shopping experience, never a separate design.

### Color Philosophy

Oat Cream (`#F6F0E7`) is the paper-like base, creating a calm surface for long-form product storytelling. Roast Brown (`#3B241C`) anchors typography and primary actions with the warmth of roasted coffee. Apricot (`#E58A55`) appears only at moments of choice, such as a selected roast or a purchase action, so it feels purposeful rather than decorative. Sage Leaf (`#82917B`) provides an honest counterweight for origin and fulfilment cues.

### Layout Paradigm

The website uses a **split editorial rail**. A narrow information rail holds navigation, cart status, and small labels. The main field carries oversized type, product imagery, and large section blocks. On mobile, the rail compresses into a sticky top bar and the content becomes a single readable vertical sequence.

### Signature Elements

1. Fine horizontal rules that separate editorial moments from shopping moments.
2. A rising-sun-and-bean mark used as a large, simple graphic motif.
3. Apricot dot markers and small all-caps labels that guide progression through the page.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should confirm, not distract. Product selection changes the chosen control clearly, cart additions trigger a concise cart tray, and links smoothly move the reader to the relevant shopping section. Any unavailable commerce action communicates that this is a concept prototype through a small notification.

### Animation

Use modest 160–220ms opacity and transform transitions for button presses, cart tray entry, and product selection. Sections may rise in with a short stagger when first viewed, but motion must respect reduced-motion preferences and never slow browsing.

### Typography System

**DM Serif Display** is used for large statements, product names, and emotive editorial lines. **Inter** handles utility text, product metadata, prices, navigation, and controls. Serif text should be large and sparse; interface text should remain compact, high-contrast, and scan-friendly.

### Brand Essence

**Morrow & Roast is a thoughtfully edited specialty coffee shop for people who want a better daily brew without a complicated buying process.**

Personality: **warm, deliberate, welcoming.**

### Brand Voice

Headlines should be calm, sensory, and direct; CTAs should be precise and action-led; microcopy should be reassuring without overpromising.

Examples: “Find the roast that fits your morning.” and “Choose your grind, then we’ll take it from there.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The wordmark uses a custom-feeling DM Serif Display setting, paired with a graphic sunrise intersecting a single coffee-bean silhouette. The symbol is designed to remain legible as an app icon and a header mark.

### Signature Brand Color

**Apricot (`#E58A55`)** is the ownable signal colour used for selections, cart counts, and the strongest calls to action.

## Style Decisions

- The desktop experience uses a narrow editorial rail for section indexing, cart status, and brand navigation; the 375px mobile layout collapses this rail into the compact top bar.
- The rising-sun-and-bean mark appears as a large editorial stamp within the page, not only as a header icon.
- The coffee shelf uses uneven, rule-led product sequencing at desktop widths so it reads as an edited coffee journal spread before it reads as a standard catalogue.
- Apricot is reserved for active choices, purchase moments, the cart count, and concise editorial progression cues.
